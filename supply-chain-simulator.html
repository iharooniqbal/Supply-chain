Supply Chain Disruption & Loss Simulator

Do you ship food that can go bad? This tool tests your plan thousands of times on the computer. It shows how much money you may lose from late trucks, warm temperatures and price changes, and which action saves the most.

How to use this tool (30 seconds)
  1. Set your situation. Use the sliders on the left. If you are not sure, leave them as they are.
  2. Press “Run the test”. The computer pretends to send thousands of shipments. It takes a few seconds.
  3. Read the table. Each row is one action you could take. Green means you save money compared with doing nothing.
  4. Click or tap a row to see its picture. The picture shows how often small and big losses happen.
  5. Change a slider and run again to see what happens when things get worse or better.

Try this: move “How often problems happen” to the right, then press the button. Watch how “Send 36 hours early” starts to save money when problems are common.

What do the words mean?
Loss
Money you lose compared with a perfect trip. It includes spoiled food, late fees, a lower selling price and the cost of any action you take.
Average loss
What you lose on a normal shipment, if you look at many shipments.
Worst 5% / worst 1%
Some trips go very badly. These numbers show the average loss on the worst 1 trip in 20, or the worst 1 trip in 100. Smaller is safer.
Chance food spoils
How many shipments out of 100 arrive too old to sell properly.
Money saved
How much less you lose on an average shipment than if you did nothing. The “±” shows how sure the answer is.
Reefer
A truck or container with a fridge or freezer.

Step 1: Set your situation

Example shipment: 20 tonnes of food worth $4,000 per tonne, 3 days of travel, and the food lasts about 10 days in a fridge. These numbers are only examples. Use your own real numbers before making real decisions.

Step 2: Compare your actions (click or tap a row to see its picture)

All numbers are dollars per shipment. Green = you save money compared with doing nothing. Red = you lose more money. Every action is tested on the same pretend shipments, so the comparison is fair.
